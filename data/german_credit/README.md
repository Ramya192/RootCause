# German Credit data

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `german.data`, `german.doc` | The raw Statlog German Credit files, unmodified, from https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/144/statlog+german+credit+data (donated by Hans Hofmann, University of Hamburg, 1994). 1,000 loan applicants at a German bank; the outcome is 1 = good (700) / 2 = bad (300). `german.doc` is the codebook. The UCI metadata does not state a license, so check the repository page before redistributing. |
| `credit.csv` | Derived by `scripts/prepare_german_credit.py`: coded categories decoded into readable levels, an `applicant_id` (row order), and the outcome `default` (1 = bad). The real data. |
| `credit_semi_synthetic.csv` | **Real applicants, simulated outcome.** Same columns as `credit.csv`. Generated by `scripts/generate_semi_synthetic_credit_data.py` from `german_credit_semi_synthetic_scm` in `rootcause/evaluation/scms.py`: every covariate (including the lending levers) is a bootstrap draw of the real applicants, and only `default` is simulated, from a logistic model with *planted* lever effects. Not a claim about what causes default. |

## What the real data can and cannot support

- **Observational.** Nobody was randomized to a loan term or amount, so there is no experimental reference. The pipeline's numbers on `credit.csv` are associations adjusted for the measured columns; unmeasured drivers of both loan terms and default (income, the loan officer's judgement) are not blocked.
- **Selection.** The outcome is only observed for loans that were granted. Applicants who were refused are absent.
- **Vintage and provenance.** Data from the early 1990s at one German bank; the original documentation is thin.

## Choices made in `prepare_german_credit.py`

- `credit_amount` is rescaled to thousands of DM (`credit_amount_kdm`) so that a one-unit change in an effect estimate is meaningful.
- "Personal status and sex" is a single coded attribute; only `female` (code A92, 310 applicants) is recoverable, and the file has no single women. It is not a clean sex variable.
- `purpose`: the four rarest categories (domestic appliances, repairs, retraining, others; 55 applicants together) are grouped as `other`.
- `young` = age under 25 (149 applicants). It is the sensitive attribute Stage 6 checks. Default rate 40.9% for young applicants against 28.1% for everyone else, a ratio of 0.69 against the four-fifths threshold of 0.8.
- 963 of 1,000 applicants are foreign workers, so a fairness check on that attribute rests on 37 people and is unstable.

## Semi-synthetic outcome model

```
default ~ Bernoulli(sigmoid(-4.649 + 0.281*young + 1.3*foreign_worker - 0.15*employment_step
                            + checking + credit_history + savings + housing + other_plans terms
                            + 0.03*duration_months + 0.10*credit_amount_kdm + 0.30*installment_rate))
```

The covariate terms are the real data's logistic-regression coefficients, rounded. The intercept and the `young` term are solved so the simulation reproduces the real file's default rate for applicants under 25 and for everyone else. The three lever coefficients (0.03, 0.10, 0.30) are chosen to be about the size of the real association; they are the planted truth the harness scores against. Because the covariates are real and the levers are correlated with them and with each other (duration and amount correlate at 0.63), a method has to adjust properly to recover the planted effects.
